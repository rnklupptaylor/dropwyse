Notation & model

Symbols & contact angles

Contact angle δ; interface tensions γsg, γsl, γlg; roughness Ar; solid fraction fsolid; liquid density ρL; viscosity η.

Subscripts smooth and rough follow the Wenzel and Cassie–Baxter equations. The annotation is measured through the liquid. Surface textures are enlarged so their geometry can be seen.

δA and δR are advancing and receding thresholds. A pinned contact line can have intermediate angles. The droplet shows current angles δup (uphill) and δdown (downhill); these are not automatically equal to the thresholds. Sw is the spreading coefficient and α is surface tilt.

Filling profiles impose the material contact angle at the moving wall. Their signed curvature pressure is separate from the applied load. They illustrate invasion; the app does not solve its flow speed or internal pressure field.

A teaching model with explicit limits

A spherical cap sets the level shape and 3D volume. A bounded, volume-preserving shear gives tilted drops different uphill and downhill angles. After roll-off, additional shape forcing is reduced; nearly spherical, highly repellent drops deform only slightly. Gravity, retention and an illustrative viscous/contact-line drag law set sliding. This is not a full Young–Laplace/Navier–Stokes solution.

Wenzel uses extruded rectangular grooves: Ar = 1 + 2h/pitch. Cassie uses square post caps: fsolid = (cap width/pitch)2. The same geometry drives the main surface and enlarged view. The macrocap volume neglects the much smaller filled texture volume.

Pinning defects are illustrative; the specified hysteresis sets retention. A 2D vented-slit model predicts local support, depinning and floor touchdown for suspended textures. It includes drop capillary pressure, a hydrostatic estimate and extra liquid-to-gas pressure. Defects and air compression are excluded. Retained, immiscible lubricant remains an assumption.

For solid surfaces, the 12° hysteresis default is illustrative, not measured material data. Advancing and receding thresholds are placed symmetrically around the reference angle, with bounds near 0° and 180°.

Reference & data

Young describes ideal wetting; Wenzel and Cassie–Baxter describe selected rough-surface states. Young–Laplace describes capillary pressure. The OWRK approximation supplies editable initial interfacial energies.

Material values are rounded, illustrative room-temperature presets. Initial pair energies use OWRK non-polar and polar components. All energies remain editable.

SLIPS hysteresis

McHale et al. report individual-drop hysteresis below 1°. The 0.5° default is a representative assumption, not a measured value for each liquid. Variation between droplets in their angle tables is not hysteresis.

What does extra pressure mean?

For example: a pressurised liquid reservoir above the texture, with the gas beneath it vented. Raising ambient pressure equally on both sides has no effect here.

0 Pa means no extra loading. The curved drop already produces capillary pressure. A small hydrostatic estimate is added separately; tilting is not the same as applying this extra pressure.

R and h refer to the candidate suspended drop. This is a quasistatic approximation. The control does not simulate an impact.
